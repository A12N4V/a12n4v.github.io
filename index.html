III  ·  Inspirations

The lineage

Cybernetics, semiotics and the mathematics of form: the people this work stands on. Each is drawn as a small machine, and most of the machines recurse.

i

Cybernetics

1926 – 2002

Stafford Beer

“The purpose of a system is what it does.”

Brain of the Firm, 1972

1894 – 1964

Norbert Wiener

“We are not stuff that abides, but patterns that perpetuate themselves.”

Cybernetics, 1948

1903 – 1972

W. Ross Ashby

“Only variety can destroy variety.”

An Introduction to Cybernetics, 1956

1911 – 2002

Heinz von Foerster

“Act always so as to increase the number of choices.”

Observing Systems, 1981

ii

Semiotics

1839 – 1914

Charles S. Peirce

“We think only in signs.”

Collected Papers, 1931 – 58

1857 – 1913

Ferdinand de Saussure

“In language there are only differences.”

Course in General Linguistics, 1916

1864 – 1944

Jakob von Uexküll

Every organism lives inside its own Umwelt, a world made of the signs it can read.

A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans, 1934

1932 – 2016

Umberto Eco

“Semiotics is in principle the discipline studying everything which can be used in order to lie.”

A Theory of Semiotics, 1976

iii

Form & computation

1912 – 1954

Alan Turing

Two chemicals, reacting and diffusing, are enough to break symmetry into spots and stripes.

The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis, 1952

1903 – 1957

John von Neumann

A machine that carries its own description can build a copy of itself.

Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata, 1966

1860 – 1948

D'Arcy Thompson

The form of an object is “a diagram of forces”.

On Growth and Form, 1917

1852 – 1934

Santiago Ramón y Cajal

Called pyramidal neurons “the mysterious butterflies of the soul”.

Textura del sistema nervioso, 1899 – 1904

IV  ·  Opera

The work

From the first questions in Delhi, to language models wired into everyday tools, to instruments for living systems, to Homonin.

I

The questions

It started in Delhi with biology, mathematics and code, and one question: can a body be written down precisely enough to compute?

II

Integrations

Language models, wired into the tools people already live in.

Mail

Semaphora

A mailing site with a model in the loop: mail written, sorted and answered alongside a language model.

Mail · Language models

Version control

Kula

A replacement for git that keeps model context with the code, so every repository can hand a model what it needs to work on it.

Version control · Model context

III

Works

Instruments for reading living systems: the brain, the heart, an ecosystem, and the literature about all of them.

Neurology

Sema

EEG analysis: rhythms, spectra and events across channels, read as signs of what the brain is doing.

EDF · BIDS · NWB

Cardiology

Cardiacity

The heart as a signal. ECG in; rhythm, intervals and variability out, each tied back to the beat it came from.

WFDB · EDF · HL7 aECG

Ecology

Pranoma

Rates how critical an organism is to its ecosystem, by how many relations it holds and how much each of them matters.

Darwin Core · GBIF · GraphML

From HomoninLiterature

Discover

Medical news, synthesised: each story is written from many sources at once, and shows how many it drew on.

Specialty feeds · Source counts

From HomoninReference

Database

A technical reference browser for the proteome, the genome and drugs, built to be read like data rather than pages.

Proteome · Genome · Drugs

From HomoninResearch

Notes

A block-based research workspace where notes become a knowledge graph, co-edited with a local medical model.

Knowledge graph · MedGemma

IV

Now

Everything above, in one place.

LiveWorkspace

Homonin

A cyber-medical research workspace: the medical literature, structured reference data and a knowledge graph in one surface, with a local model reasoning over all of it.

Discover · Database · Notes

V  ·  Post-AI

The post-AI era

Models can now write, see and speak. What they can't do on their own is stay true to the body. The work above is built to keep that loop closed.
